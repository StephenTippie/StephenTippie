## How I got here

I did not come into building through a conventional software-only path. My background was forged in environments where ambiguity, urgency, handoffs, friction, and changing capacity were normal parts of the job.

That includes high-severity incident handling, executive escalations, cross-functional problem-solving, and process repair, alongside independent business and operations work involving planning, budgeting, workflow design, and vendor coordination.

That history shapes how I think about systems. People lose context, teams inherit incomplete information, priorities shift midstream, and work often has to continue under imperfect conditions. I care about tools and artifacts that help people regain their footing without starting over from scratch.

## How I build

I build for real human conditions, not idealized users.

That usually means:

* clarity over hype
* structure only when it improves usability
* documentation that supports action, not just description
* workflows that survive interruption
* tools that are easier to re-enter later
* artifacts that stay legible, reusable, and grounded over time
* boundaries between what is public, what is private, and what should remain intentionally scoped
* designs that acknowledge pressure, ambiguity, and shifting capacity as normal realities

A lot of my work sits at the intersection of workflow design, documentation architecture, structured communication, and practical systems engineering. I care less about complexity for its own sake and more about whether something stays useful under real conditions.

## Current direction

Right now, I’m focused on building small, structured, reusable systems that help people preserve continuity, recover their footing after interruption, and turn rough or fragmented thinking into usable artifacts.

The through-line is simple: reduce unnecessary friction, protect continuity, and design systems that hold up when reality is messy.
